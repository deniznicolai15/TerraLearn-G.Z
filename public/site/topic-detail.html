What is a Forest Ecosystem?

Forests in the Philippines are diverse and vital. From the dipterocarp forests of Luzon and Mindanao, to the mangroves protecting coastal communities, and the montane forests of the Cordilleras, these ecosystems sustain life, regulate climate, and protect communities. They are often called the lungs of the Earth because they produce oxygen and store carbon, making them essential for both local and global balance.

The Sierra Madre mountain range, the longest in the Philippines, is a critical forest ecosystem that protects Luzon from typhoons and provides water to millions.

Structure of Philippine Forest Ecosystems

Forests here are layered systems, each with distinct ecological roles:

Emergent Layer

Towering trees like lauan and apitong rise above all others, capturing sunlight. Birds of prey such as the Philippine eagle soar here.

Canopy

A dense roof of leaves that shelters hornbills, fruit bats, and monkeys. It creates a humid microclimate essential for orchids and epiphytes.

Understory

Shaded shrubs and small trees, home to reptiles, amphibians, and medicinal plants used in traditional healing.

Forest Floor

Dark, damp, and nutrient-rich. Decomposers recycle organic matter, while wild boars and deer forage among fallen leaves.
